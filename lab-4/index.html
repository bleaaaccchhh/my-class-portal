Early life and Radiohead

Jonny Greenwood was born Jonathan Richard Guy Greenwood on 5 November 1971 in Oxford, England. He is the younger brother of Colin Greenwood, who plays bass in Radiohead. The band formed in Abingdon, Oxfordshire, in the 1980s, and Jonny joined as a teenager, becoming its lead guitarist and keyboardist.

Radiohead has released nine studio albums, from Pablo Honey in 1993 to A Moon Shaped Pool in 2016. Across those records, Greenwood's playing ranges from the jagged guitar of the band's early work to the electronics and orchestral arrangements of its later albums.

Composer for film and orchestra

Outside the band, Greenwood has built a second career as a composer. He served as composer in residence with the BBC Concert Orchestra in the mid-2000s, and his first film score was for the documentary Bodysong (2003). He went on to score There Will Be Blood (2007), the first of several collaborations with director Paul Thomas Anderson.

His scores for Phantom Thread (2017) and The Power of the Dog (2021) were each nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Score. Other films he has scored include We Need to Talk About Kevin, The Master, Inherent Vice, You Were Never Really Here, and Spencer.

Instruments and approach

Greenwood trained as a violist and is known for playing the ondes Martenot, an early electronic instrument played with a keyboard and a ring worn on the finger. He also uses Max, a visual programming environment for music, to build instruments and processes for his compositions.

He often mixes acoustic orchestral writing with electronics, which gives his scores a recognizable texture.
