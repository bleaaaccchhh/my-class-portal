Why

Accessibility is not a niche feature. It is how a site works for people.

It reaches more people than you think

About one in four US adults has a disability, according to the CDC. Many more face temporary or situational limits: a broken arm, a bright sidewalk, a loud venue.

It makes things better for everyone

Sidewalk curb cuts were built for wheelchairs and now help strollers, bikes, and luggage. Captions started as an access tool and are now how many people watch video on mute.

The web is mostly failing

The WebAIM Million study finds detectable accessibility failures on the vast majority of top home pages every year. Low contrast text and missing alt text lead the list.

It is also the law and good business

Web accessibility lawsuits under the ADA are filed in the thousands each year, and accessible sites tend to rank better and convert more users.
